Choosing the Right Structure for Your Business.

The structure of a business affects how it is operated, taxed, accounted for and administered. Choosing between operating as a sole trader and establishing a company should therefore be considered as a business decision, not simply a registration exercise.

Starting a business involves more than choosing a name and beginning to trade. One of the first questions an entrepreneur needs to consider is how the business should be structured.

In Cyprus, a business may operate through different structures depending on its activities, ownership, risk profile, expected growth and compliance requirements.

Sole trader or company?

A sole trader structure is generally associated with an individual carrying on business in their own capacity. A company, by contrast, is a separate legal entity with its own rights, obligations and records.

The distinction can affect taxation, accounting, liability, administration, financing and the way profits are ultimately extracted from the business.

01

Sole Trader

The individual operates the business directly and is responsible for the associated business activity and obligations.

02

Company

The business operates through a separate legal entity with its own accounting, reporting and compliance responsibilities.

Liability and risk

One important consideration is the nature and level of risk associated with the business.

A limited liability company generally provides a legal separation between the company and its shareholders, subject to the applicable law and circumstances.

This does not mean that incorporation removes all personal risk. Directors and individuals can still have personal responsibilities and liabilities in particular circumstances.

Entrepreneurs should therefore consider the actual risks of their activities rather than treating incorporation as a universal solution.

Accounting requirements

The structure of a business also influences the accounting and reporting work required.

A company needs its own accounting records and must comply with the applicable corporate and tax requirements. Financial information needs to be maintained separately from the personal finances of its owners.

For a growing business, maintaining proper records from the beginning can make it easier to monitor profitability, manage cash flow and prepare for future reporting obligations.

Tax is only one part of the decision

Tax is naturally an important consideration when comparing structures, but it should not be examined in isolation.

From 2026, the Cyprus corporate income-tax rate is 15%. Individuals are subject to progressive income tax under the new 2026 tax bands.

The appropriate structure therefore depends on the individual's circumstances, expected income, business activities, plans for retaining or distributing profits and other relevant factors.

There is no universal structure

The most appropriate structure depends on the nature of the business, the people involved, expected activity, risk and long-term objectives.

Growth and future plans

A structure that appears appropriate when a business is very small may not remain appropriate as the business develops.

Entrepreneurs should consider questions such as:

  • Will the business employ staff?
  • Will external investors become involved?
  • Will the business enter into significant contracts?
  • Will the business operate internationally?
  • Will profits be reinvested into the business?
  • Will ownership eventually be transferred or shared?

International entrepreneurs

For entrepreneurs relocating to Cyprus or establishing a Cyprus business while maintaining international interests, the analysis can become more complex.

Issues such as tax residence, the location of management and business activity, foreign income, double-taxation considerations and the structure of ownership may need to be considered.

A business structure should therefore be considered together with the individual's wider personal and international circumstances.

What should you consider before incorporating?

Before deciding on a structure, an entrepreneur should ideally have a clear understanding of:

  • The nature of the proposed business.
  • Expected turnover and profitability.
  • The level of commercial risk.
  • Whether other owners or investors will participate.
  • The likely accounting and compliance workload.
  • The intended use of business profits.
  • Whether the business will have international activity.

The practical takeaway

Choosing a business structure should be treated as part of the business planning process.

The objective is not simply to select the structure with the lowest immediate tax cost. It is to choose a structure that makes sense for the business, supports its future plans and allows its accounting, tax and legal obligations to be managed properly.

Reviewing the options before registration can be significantly more useful than discovering later that the original structure no longer fits the business.

General information

This article is provided for general information purposes only and does not constitute legal, tax or accounting advice. The appropriate business structure depends on individual circumstances and professional advice should be obtained before establishing or restructuring a business.
